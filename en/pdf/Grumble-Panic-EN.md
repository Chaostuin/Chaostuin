## Chapter 1 – The Apple Drop

Olivia stood in the schoolyard with a half-eaten apple in her hand.

"Not the trash bin again," she sighed. "Teacher says compost is good for the garden."

She glanced around and slipped through the gate to the compost heap behind the school.

There, she stopped.

It was... strange.

The compost heap looked different than usual.

There were things on it that didn't belong there at all: a piece of plastic toy, a plastic straw, something that looked like a glittering scrap of gift ribbon.

It didn't smell like usual — not like soil or leaves, but sour and heavy, as if something was rotting that shouldn't be there.

Olivia frowned.

"This isn't right," she whispered.

Carefully, she let the apple core fall.

On the moment the piece of apple hit the pile, everything began to glow. A flash! Bright green! *Floop!*

The ground disappeared beneath her feet.

Olivia fell — not hard, more like she was on a slippery slide. She spun, tumbled, and... *ploop!*

She landed softly in something that smelled of old cauliflower and mud pies.

"Ugh," she mumbled, scrambling to her feet.
"Where am I!?"

"Welcome to the Compost Factory!" called a cheerful voice.

In front of her stood a small little man.

He wore a coat of leaves, glasses made of corn kernels, and a hat of tree bark.

"I am **Master Mulch**," he said with a bow. "And you have just opened the secret door... to the life beneath your feet."

Olivia looked around. Her eyes went wide.

Everywhere, life was teeming:

Worms twirled through glistening tunnels, woodlice pulled little carts full of leaf scraps, millipedes unrolled cables between fungal houses, springtails hopped like tiny springs over everything, and bacteria danced in gently steaming little jars.

"Is this... real?" whispered Olivia.

"As real as compost in your sock!" chuckled Master Mulch.

But suddenly Olivia wrinkled her nose.

"Ugh! What stinks so badly here?"

Master Mulch looked worriedly behind him.

There, a cloud of dark smoke curled up from a heap of grey muck.

"Hmm…" he mumbled. "It's worse than I thought."

## Chapter 2 – Compost Tricks

Master Mulch led Olivia to a large, creaking machine.

"This," he said proudly, "is the Green Mill."

Standing beside it was Willy the Worm, wearing a bent little helmet and a tool belt around his wriggly body.

"Set it to fifty percent brown, Willy!" called Master Mulch.

"Coming right up, chief!" squeaked Willy, turning a rusty lever.

"Green stuff — like vegetables, fruit, and grass — is full of moisture and energy," explained Master Mulch.

"But too much green and you get a swampy soup! That's why we add dry brown material: dry leaves, cardboard, straw…"

"So compost is actually a kind of... vegetable lasagna?" asked Olivia.

"Ha! Exactly! Layer of green, layer of brown," laughed Willy.

"And if something goes wrong," said Master Mulch mysteriously, "the inspection gnomes stand ready. They live deep below, in the Quality Control Room. Always ready to step in."

The machine started to thump and turn. It went *WRONK! PLONK!* and *PFFFT!*

But then...

A rustling sound from the corner.

"Pete the Woodlouse?" called Master Mulch.

A tiny creature with a magnifying glass and a detective hat stumbled forward. He rolled a dirty, stinking bag out of the pile.

"Plastic... batteries... chip wrappers?! Who does something like that?!" he squeaked indignantly.

Master Mulch frowned deeply.

"That, Olivia," he said gravely, "is forbidden waste. And I fear... it's the work of an old enemy."

Suddenly a voice rumbled through the tunnel.

A dark, sticky voice.

"Say goodbye to your compost, Mulchy…"

Olivia turned around and held her breath.

Out of the shadows grew a repulsive mountain of junk.

He had eyes like marbles. And a grin of plastic forks.

He slurped, sputtered, and shuffled closer.

**Grumble!**

---

Olivia stared at the trash heap steaming like a stinking soup.

The air stung her nose, and her eyes watered a little.

"So… that's Grumble?" she whispered. Her voice sounded small.

Master Mulch nodded gravely.

"He started out as just one carelessly discarded plastic bag. But every time someone throws something wrong into the compost, he grows bigger."

Olivia swallowed. She watched Grumble move slowly forward, like a slimy dragon made of sandwich bags, batteries, and rotten fish sticks.

Everywhere he crawled, he left a trail of goo and grime behind.

She couldn't take her eyes off the monster.

His body heaved and squelched as if it were breathing. Here and there, forks, straws, and torn candy wrappers stuck out of his back like spines.

He growled softly, a wet, bubbling sound.

Olivia felt her stomach clench.

"*And it's getting worse!*" squeaked Babs the Bacteria in a cracking voice. She wobbled closer, wrapped in a far-too-large doctor's coat hanging crookedly over her shoulders. In her hand trembled a thermometer, as if it were cold itself.

"I just measured it with my snot-o-meter," she said worriedly. "It's way too wet and sour here. We bacteria are exhausted!"

She pulled out a messy little notebook full of scribbles and stains. "Look, even my slime score is nearly zero!"

She looked at the others with wide eyes. "And without us… no compost. Just stinking mud!"

"Grumble makes everything soggy," sighed Willy the Worm. "I've already slipped in his sticky trails three times."

"And my little legs get stuck to it!" squeaked an angry springtail wearing a helmet of rose petals.

"He clogs up the air holes!" called Moldina, balancing on a little mushroom stool. "Without air, we fungi start wobbling like moldy pudding!"

Olivia clenched her fists.

She felt anger bubbling up, but also… sadness.

"But… why is he doing this?" she asked, her voice cracking.

Master Mulch raised a little twig into the air like a magic wand.

"Grumble lives off what people do wrong," he said gravely. "The more wrong waste — the stronger he gets. He wants to turn the compost into a stinking mud pit where nobody wants to live anymore."

Olivia looked down at her shoes.

She thought of the compost heap behind the school. How she'd also seen strange things lying there — a glittery ribbon, an empty chip bag. Hadn't *she*, back then, done… nothing?

She bit her lip. Not now.

"But… can't we just… scoop him up?" she asked, already knowing it wouldn't be that simple.

"Dangerous! He slurps up scoops and spits them back out as tape bombs!"

Olivia took a step back.

Then she looked at Master Mulch.

"But… you're the boss here, right? The great Compost Master? Can't you stop him?"

Mulch sighed, and his bark hat sank a little lower.

"Long ago, maybe," he said softly. "But I am made of compost myself. And Grumble… he's everything that doesn't belong in it. He weakens me. If I get too close to him, I literally fall apart. Twig by twig."

He looked at her, his eyes like wet leaves.

"But you… you come from above. You see what people do. You can turn this around."

Olivia squeezed her eyes shut.

Thought hard. Very hard.

The stink, the mess, the panic… It had all started because nobody had really paid attention.

Then she opened her eyes. Determined.

"Then we have to be smart," she said. "Smarter than Grumble.

If he lives off wrong stuff… then we simply stop feeding him."

"We have to work together," said Olivia.

Derek the Millipede nodded with all his little feet at once. "Just tell us what to do, I'm fast *and* tidy."

The springtails formed long lines, passing messages from one to the next in little hops.

A silence fell.

Then Master Mulch leaned toward her and smiled.

"Now you're thinking like a true Compost Guardian, Olivia."

Babs the Bacteria grabbed a scrap of cardboard and began sketching with an old pencil.

"We'll make a plan. And fast, before he clogs the air pipes!"

Olivia looked once more at the trash heap.

Grumble slowly turned toward her.

His body shook and creaked.

And then… he grinned. With teeth made of butter tub lids.

## Chapter 3 – Tunnels and Air

"It stinks worse here than a skunk with garlic socks!" called Willy the Worm, straightening his helmet.

Olivia pinched her nose shut. The air was so thick and sour it made her freckles feel like they were curling.

"The compost is getting clogged," sighed Moldina, a tall, purple fungus with a straw hat and a voice like a kettle whistle. She wobbled on her rotting mushroom stool and pulled a little sachet of lavender from her hat. "In case I faint from the smell."

"So… compost really needs to be able to breathe?" asked Olivia.

Master Mulch nodded. "Absolutely! Air makes sure everything breaks down properly. Without air, the good little critters get tired… and the naughty bacteria wake up. Then our nice heap turns into a stinking soup."

"Then it's time for action!" called Willy. "Come on, Olivia, let's dig air tunnels. I dig faster than a worm racing toward a watermelon seed!"

Olivia dropped to her knees. Together they started digging. The compost felt warm and crumbly, but sticky and heavy in places.

Every time they dug a nice tunnel, there came a sound:

**"Squiiirrrch… sploorp-plop!"**

and a thick, slimy mass would push into their tunnels.

"That's Grumble!" cried Willy, his helmet slipping sideways. "He's stuffing our tunnels full of junk!"

"He's doing it on purpose," said Olivia, fists clenched.

Pete the Woodlouse came running up, out of breath.

"The left side of the heap is about to cave in! Without air, splat! Flat as a pancake!"

Moldina looked at Olivia. "The air needs to be able to flow like a breeze on a summer day. Can you think of something?"

Olivia thought. She looked at the heap, at the messy tunnels… and at her dirty hands.

Then she had an idea.

"What if we make a fake tunnel? A decoy passage that Grumble throws himself into. Meanwhile, we dig the real air passages on the other side!"

Willy's eyes lit up. "Ha! Smarter than a compost beetle with a puzzle book!"

They drew a map in the compost, agreed on secret signals, and started digging.

They gave the decoy tunnel extra noise, plopping sounds, and glittery cardboard.

In the distance, Grumble growled.

But Olivia grinned and whispered:

"Air is life. And we… are going to breathe instead of stink."

## Chapter 4 – The Brown Mission

The compost heap sounded like it had a cold.

"*Snrrrggggl-blop…*"

Olivia pulled a disgusted face.

"It's like someone spilled snot into the soup!"

"Too wet," sighed Master Mulch. "There's too much soggy mush in the heap. And far too little brown."

"What do you mean by brown?" asked Olivia.

"Dry stuff," squeaked Babs the Bacteria, crawling out of a slimy apple lump. "Leaves, straw, cardboard... everything that crunches when you squeeze it!"

"That keeps the heap dry and airy," said Master Mulch. "Without brown… we become a stinking soup mountain!"

Olivia raised her fist.

"Then I'll go find brown! Who's coming?"

"Me!" buzzed a voice.

Frits the Fly came zooming in on sharp zigzags. His glasses sat crooked, one lens held together with tape, and one of his little shoes hung loose off his leg. His wings wobbled like plastic bags in the wind.

"I know the way to the Brown Hall!" he squeaked proudly. "Got lost there just the day before yesterday, so now I know exactly where it *isn't*. Follow me!"

Along the way he fluttered back and forth like a stunt pilot. They passed rotten broccoli, lopsided pumpkin seeds, and a chain of beetles wearing raincoats.

Frits made the occasional enthusiastic loop-the-loop and called, "Don't mind the smell, that's just my deodorant, garlic juice."

The further they went, the drier it got, and the better it smelled.

"Ah, here it is," said Frits, making an elegant turn and bumping into a mushroom. "The Brown Hall!"

Under a mountain of crunchy leaves lay a stockpile of dry material, neatly stacked the way a squirrel would do it. Olivia climbed onto a hay bale.

"Perfect! Come on, let's scoop it up!"

But just as she was loading up her arms...

**KLOD-KLATSSJJJJ!**

Something shiny rolled inside.

"What's *that* now?" asked Frits.

"Wait a second," said Olivia, "that's not real brown material… That's fake cardboard! Fake-brown!"

"Fake-brown?" Frits looked as if his glasses had fogged up.

Pete the Woodlouse came running, panting, with a magnifying glass.

"A trap from Grumble! He tricks people into throwing printed, plastic-coated cardboard into the compost. And it ends up here!"

"Not everything that looks brown is actually good," murmured Olivia. "Time for a check."

Together they made a list:

**Good:**
Dry leaves
Dry twigs
Straw
Cardboard without a plastic coating

**Bad:**
Colored flyers
Eggshells with glitter (!)

"Glitter belongs in a fairy ballet," sighed Olivia, "not in the compost!"

Arms full of real brown treasure, they ran back to the heap.

Master Mulch stood ready with a pitchfork.

"Quick! Throw it in!"

They scattered leaves, tore cardboard into strips, and let it drift down like autumn rain.

Slowly, the wet sludge turned into crumbly, fragrant earth.

Willy sniffed deeply. "Say… do I smell... forest soil?"

"That's compost in top form," said Master Mulch, his eyes twinkling.

Olivia looked toward the middle of the heap.

There stood Grumble. He growled. But… he was smaller than before.

"He's losing power," she whispered.

"But he's not gone yet," grumbled Frits.

"Then," said Olivia firmly, "it's time for… a trap."

## Chapter 5 – The Grumble Trap

"A trap? For Grumble?" squeaked Willy the Worm.

"He's bigger than a garbage truck full of peanut butter and socks!"

"Exactly why," said Olivia firmly. "We'll build a fake heap. A decoy pile, full of stinking junk he loves."

Master Mulch scratched his mossy beard.

"Hmm… a junk buffet to fool him…"

"And then we lure him into the Quality Control Room," said Olivia. "He'll get stuck there. Among the sieves and magnifying glasses. Nothing left to snack on!"

Everyone helped out.

Pete the Woodlouse gathered shiny cardboard and empty cookie wrappers.

Frits the Fly brought broken batteries (with thick gloves!).

Moldina tossed on a few bits of old pizza for the smell.

Willy threw in a gnawed chicken wing.

They stacked it all onto a big heap. It bubbled, steamed, and glowed faintly.

"It smells like someone left their sandwiches in a backpack for three years!" chuckled Willy.

"Perfect," said Olivia with a wink.

Beneath the Bait Pile lay a secret tunnel. It led straight to the *Quality Control Room* — a gleaming white space full of magnifying glasses, inspection gnomes, and beeping screens.

"As soon as he jumps onto the pile," whispered Olivia, "we'll pull this rope. And then floop! He'll be gone!"

"And then what?" asked Babs the Bacteria.

"Then he won't get anything new to grow from," said Master Mulch. "No more junk. Just… order and tidiness."

Suddenly a greasy voice sounded.

"Sniiiffff... snorrrrk... what do I smell there?!"

Grumble came rolling in! Even bigger than before. On his head glinted a crown of tin cans. Around his shoulders flapped a cape stained with salad dressing.

His eyes gleamed. He saw the Bait Pile.

"So... much... junk... for me?!"

He jumped right on top of the pile.

"Pull now!" cried Olivia. *SWOOSH!* The ground beneath Grumble slid away.

He slid down, rolled, screamed something like "compost is for suckers!" and *PLOP!* — he landed right in the middle of the Quality Control Room.

The doors slammed shut.

From the corners, the inspection gnomes stormed in: little creatures with eggshell helmets and brushes at the ready.

They waved placards:

"NON-COMPOSTABLE!"
"PLASTIC ALERT!"
"CONTAMINATION!"

And with great enthusiasm, they began cleaning up the mess, whistling like garbage collectors in an orchestra.

You could hear Grumble whimpering softly as the inspection gnomes rummaged through his belly with tongs.

"This fork isn't bio!"
"This battery needs to be recycled!"
"This wrapper is fake paper!"

---

Then Olivia stared at the empty spot where Grumble had stood.

"Did we make it?" she whispered.

Willy pricked up his little ears.

The compost heap was silent.

And then —

*bloop!*
*pffrrrt!*

An air bubble popped on top of the heap.

Master Mulch smiled. "That was a happy sigh. From a heap that can finally breathe again."

## Chapter 6 – The Great Clean-Up

The air in the Compost Factory smelled… different.

Fresh. Warm. As if someone had opened a window and swapped the stink for the scent of an autumn forest after rain.

Olivia brushed a strand of compost out of her hair and looked proudly at the trap door.

**In the Quality Control Room**

"But now," said Master Mulch, "now the *real* work begins. Cleaning up! Mixing green and brown in balance! And making the compost healthy again!"

**Cleaning Up Together**

Everyone rolled up their sleeves — or legs.

- Willy the Worm loosened up tunnels that Grumble had smeared shut with sticky gunk.
- Pete the Woodlouse made two piles: one with things allowed to stay, and one with junk that had to go.
- Moldina sprinkled her airy mold powder to open up air pockets.
- Frits the Fly zoomed back and forth, calling through his megaphone voice:
  "Dry over here! Wet over there! Shovel to the left!"

Olivia got put in charge of the Mixing Team.

She called out:

"Leaves to zone 3!"
"Wet fruit mush to the left side!"
"Who's still got clean cardboard?"

"It's like a compost orchestra," she whispered.

Everyone worked at their own rhythm — but together it sounded like music.

**Dark, Crumbly, Alive**

After a few days, the heap was unrecognizable.

Where a stinking mush had once lain, there was now something else:

- Dark brown.
- Crumbly.
- Warm. Not hot, but cozy.

Olivia stuck her hand in.

It felt like a warm coat on a cold day.

And it was *alive*!

Worms wriggled contentedly, bacteria gleamed with health, and somewhere a woodlouse played a leaf like it was a trumpet.

"Is it… done now?" asked Olivia.

Master Mulch looked carefully at the heap.

He squeezed his eyes into slits and sniffed.

"Almost," he nodded. "Compost is slow and wise. A little more rest… and then the black gold is ready. Food for everything that grows!"

"And Grumble?" asked Olivia.

"We're recycling him," smiled Master Mulch. "Into a lesson.

He'll always exist a little bit. But as long as you keep explaining what does and doesn't belong… he'll stay small."

Olivia nodded.

And deep inside, she felt it:

The Compost Factory was breathing again.

## Chapter 7 – Compost Guardian Olivia

Everywhere, things crawled and teemed again — the Compost Factory came back to life like a cheerful anthill.

The Green Mill hummed again like a contented bee.

The worm tunnels bubbled, the worms danced, woodlice drove cheerfully back and forth again, millipedes ran around with tools, springtails jumped for joy, and Babs the Bacteria spun pirouettes on the air shafts as if she'd never been sick at all. The Compost Factory was healed!

Olivia sat on a bench of woven twigs, her boots covered in stains and her hair still a little messy from the action.

She looked around.

It felt good.

Not perfect. Not spotless.

But truly good. Because she had helped. Fought. Composted.

**A Little Pot of Honor**

Master Mulch shuffled over with something glimmering in his hands.

"Olivia," he said with a deep bow, "this is the first little pot of compost from the restored heap. Dark. Crumbly. Full of life."

Olivia accepted it. The little pot was small, but felt warm, as if it had just been scooped from a compost crucible.

She opened the lid. The smell made her smile: earth, autumn, adventure.

Master Mulch pulled out a freshly pressed compost leaf badge — beautifully shiny brown with a green edge.

He pinned it onto her sweater like a medal.

"From today on," he announced solemnly, "you are… Compost Guardian Olivia."

Worm streamers flew into the air.

Pete the Woodlouse threw leaves like confetti.

Frits the Fly did three dizzy loop-the-loops around her head. Even the Green Mill let out a festive puffing *PFOEFP!*

**Back to the Surface**

With a flash and a cheerful *ploop!*, Olivia was pulled upward.

She landed with a soft *pats!* on her knees — right next to the compost heap behind the school.

Exactly where it had all begun.

She stood up, brushed a stray leaf off her cheek, and looked around as if everything seemed just a little bit clearer.

Then she stepped into the classroom.

And she told the story.

About worms and tunnels.
About green and brown.
About air and love.

And most of all: about what does *not* belong in a heap that's alive.

The teacher wrote on the board:

**NO plastic. NO batteries.
YES: peels, leaves, coffee grounds.**

The children nodded. Some even looked a little bit ashamed. Olivia felt something bubbling up inside: pride. And responsibility.

**A Shadow in the Green**

That afternoon, she walked back out to the compost heap, the little pot in her hands.

She wanted to carefully scatter the rich compost as a blessing for a fresh start.

But… something glinted between the leaves.

A scrap of glitter paper.

And there — a shadow. Long. Sneaking.

Slowly withdrawing back between the branches.

Olivia narrowed her eyes.

"Grumble…" she whispered.

She set down her little pot.

And rolled up her sleeves.

"Then you know I'm here too."

Because the battle for compost wasn't over yet.

But from now on, there was a guardian by the heap.

And that guardian… was Olivia.

**THE END**
