Tending vegetables in a chaos garden

A healthy garden mostly grows by itself, but every now and then a plant needs a helping hand.

What is it?

Tending means helping your plants where needed.
A chaos garden takes care of a lot by itself: healthy soil, less drying out, more natural predators, and stronger plants. But some vegetables simply need a bit of extra guidance to stay healthy and give a good harvest.

Main benefits

  • Healthier, stronger plants
  • Bigger, better-looking harvest
  • less chance of disease
  • Better use of space
  • less energy wasted by the plant

How do you tend your plants?

  • check regularly how your plants are growing
  • tie up tall plants in time so they don't topple over
  • remove excess shoots or leaves where needed
  • thin out young plants if they're too close together
  • give support to climbing species
  • harvest regularly so plants keep producing
  • only intervene when it's truly necessary

Every vegetable is different

Some vegetables need almost no tending. Others need a bit of extra attention during the season.
  • tomatoes: removing side shoots, tying up, removing lower leaves
  • cucumbers: tying up, guiding if needed
  • climbing beans and snap peas: staking
  • peppers: sometimes tying up
  • aubergines: staking with heavy fruit, removing lower leaves
  • zucchini: removing old or diseased leaves
  • lettuce, spinach, carrots, onions, garlic, beets: no tending needed

Nuance

A chaos garden doesn't need to look perfect. A crooked plant or a leaf with a hole is not a problem. Tend your plants so they stay healthy, not so they look like they came out of a catalogue.
