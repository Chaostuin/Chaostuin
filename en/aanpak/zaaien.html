Sowing in a mulched garden

A thick mulch layer protects your soil, but makes direct sowing tricky. Fortunately, there are smart solutions for sowing successfully in a mulched garden anyway.

What's the problem

A mulch layer of 5 cm or thicker makes germination difficult because seeds end up too deep or get smothered.

🌱 1. Pre-sowing

  • Sow first in pots, seed trays or other small containers, and plant out later.
  • ✅ Ideal for: lettuce, tomatoes and many more
  • What can you pre-sow in?

🌿 2. Temporarily removing mulch

  • Push the mulch aside and sow directly into the soil. Once the seedlings are big enough, mulch again. 👉 Especially useful for root vegetables that transplant poorly.
  • ✅ Ideal for: carrots, radishes, parsnips, or a polyculture
  • Provide protection — blackbirds love to fling the surrounding mulch around.

🌻 3. Sowing through the mulch

  • Plant large seeds under the mulch, into the soil. The seedlings are strong enough to grow up through the mulch.
  • ✅ Ideal for: beans, peas, pumpkin, zucchini, corn, sunflowers.

🛒 4. Buying young plants

  • Save time by buying strong, healthy young plants from an organic grower or garden centre
  • ✅ Ideal for: tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, herbs, leeks, strawberries.

🌱 5. Sowing between the mulch

  • Make a groove or a hole in the mulch, fill it with seed compost, and sow away.
  • As the seedlings emerge and grow bigger, you can add mulch right up to the plants.
  • The difference with option 3 (sowing through the mulch) is that we don't cover the seed compost with mulch until the plants are big enough.
  • ✅ Ideal for: carrots, chard, parsnips, parsley.

Nuance

Don't pick one method for everything — use the approach that best fits the vegetable, the season, your mulch layer, and whatever is most practical at the time.
